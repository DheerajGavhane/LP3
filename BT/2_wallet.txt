Cryptocurrency is a digital form of currency that uses cryptography to secure transactions.
Unlike traditional banking systems, cryptocurrency can enable peer-to-peer transactions without requiring a bank to verify every payment.
A cryptocurrency wallet is a software application or physical device used to manage the cryptographic keys that control access to cryptocurrency.

Types of cryptocurrency wallets
1. Hot Wallet
- Connected to the internet.
- Easy to access and use.
- Suitable for frequent transactions.
- More exposed to online threats.
Examples: MetaMask and mobile cryptocurrency wallets.
2. Cold Wallet
- Stores private keys offline.
- Provides protection against many online attacks.
- Suitable for long-term storage.
- Usually requires a physical device or secure offline setup.
Examples: Hardware wallets and properly secured offline wallets.

Phishing Scams
- Scammers create fake websites, emails or messages.
- They pretend to be legitimate cryptocurrency platforms.
- They ask users to enter their wallet passwords or recovery phrases.
- They steal wallet credentials and cryptocurrency.
Example: A fake MetaMask website asking for your Secret Recovery Phrase.

2. Ponzi and Pyramid Schemes
- Scammers promise unusually high returns on cryptocurrency investments.
- They use money from new investors to pay earlier investors.
- Eventually, the scheme collapses when new investments stop.
Example: A platform promising guaranteed 20% monthly cryptocurrency returns.

3. Fake ICO and Investment Scams
- Scammers introduce fake cryptocurrency projects.
- They advertise unrealistic profits and future growth.
- Investors purchase fake tokens or send cryptocurrency to scammers.
- The scammers disappear with the funds.
ICO stands for Initial Coin Offering.

4. Giveaway and Impersonation Scams
- Scammers impersonate celebrities, companies or cryptocurrency influencers.
- They promise to double the cryptocurrency sent to them.
- Victims send cryptocurrency but never receive anything in return.
Example: "Send 1 ETH and receive 2 ETH."

Types of Cryptojacking:
1. Browser-based cryptojacking: Malicious scripts run through a web browser.
2. Malware-based cryptojacking: Malicious software is installed on the victim's device.
